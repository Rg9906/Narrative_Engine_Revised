Chapter 3: The Fractured Alibi
The storm outside Whitmore Manor seemed to mimic the rising panic within. In the drawing room, the air was thick with the scent of damp wool and old coal dust. Rain lashed against the high arched windows, casting trembling, watery shadows across the mahogany floorboards.

Detective Alfred stood by the fireplace, his gaze fixed on Laurie Whitmore. Laurie sat perfectly still, his posture impeccable, though a slight tension in his jaw betrayed his calm facade. In the corner, Lucius watched in silence, his arms crossed, while Sebastian stood near the entryway, his greatcoat still glistening with rainwater, his chest heaving slightly.

"Let us return to your timeline, Laurie," Alfred said, his voice dropping to a low, rhythmic purr. He pulled a brass pocket watch from his waistcoat, turning it over in his hand. "You claimed that during the hour of the storm—the very hour the milk bottle in the study was tampered with—you were in the library, and subsequently the study, accompanied by Miss Talia."

Laurie offered a faint, polite smile. "That is correct, Inspector. We were discussing the family archives. The rain had made us restless."

"A charming picture," Alfred murmured. He took a slow step forward. "There is only one minor complication. I have just spoken with Talia’s maid, Winnie. Winnie spent the entire night in the ante-room adjoining Miss Talia’s private bedchamber. She is quite adamant that her mistress never left her bed all evening, having taken a mild sedative for her headache at nine o'clock."

The silence in the room stretched tense and fragile.

Laurie’s eyes flicked toward the window, just for a fraction of a second, before settling back on the detective. "Winnie is a loyal servant, Inspector, but perhaps her own fatigue got the better of her. Or perhaps... she is mistaken about the time."

"Winnie's timepiece is synchronized with the grandfather clock in the hall, which I have just verified," Alfred countered smoothly. "And what of you, Sebastian? Your sudden entrance earlier was... striking. You appeared in the drawing room almost as if by magic."

Sebastian took a sharp breath, stepping forward. "I apologize if my arrival startled the household, Inspector. When I heard the glass shatter from the carriage house, I ran back across the courtyard. The mud was thick, and I nearly lost my footing twice, which is why my boots are caked in clay. The side door was bolted, so I had to circle around to the terrace doors. I burst in as quickly as my lungs would allow."

Alfred nodded, his eyes tracking the dark, wet clay clinging to Sebastian’s boots—a physical vector that finally aligned with the mud trail leading from the terrace. "A breathless run. Yes, that explains the state of your coat, Mr. Whitmore."

Alfred turned his attention back to the table in the center of the room. Resting on a silver tray was the glass milk bottle, its wax seal crudely hacked away, a faint, bitter-almond scent drifting from its rim.

"So," Alfred sighed, looking between the brothers. "We have a bottle of milk laced with cyanide sitting in a study that was supposed to be empty. We have a young lady asleep in her chambers while her cousin claims her as an alibi. And we have a shattered window with no culprit in sight. Someone in this house is spinning a very delicate web. But webs, gentlemen, are incredibly easy to tear."
