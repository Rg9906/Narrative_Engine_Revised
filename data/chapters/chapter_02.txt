Chapter 2: The Silver Gate

Alice woke as the morning light filtered through the dense canopy of the Thornwood. The stream was cold, and she splashed her face to chase away the lingering fragments of her dreams. In those dreams, her father had stood before the Silver Gate, holding the worn leather journal.

She packed her camp quickly and set off on the north road. By midday, the hills gave way to a barren plain of dark stone. And there, standing against the gray sky, was the Silver Gate. It was not a physical wall, but two massive pillars of shimmering liquid silver, hummed with a low vibration that resonated in her palms.

"I see you made it," a voice said behind her.

Alice spun around, her hand dropping to the hilt of her small dagger. Out of the shadows of the pillars stepped the old woman from Millhaven, no longer peeling apples, but carrying a staff of dark wood.

"You shouldn't have come, Alice," the woman said quietly. "Thomas was right. This gate has been closed for an age, and the key your father sought was never meant to be turned."
