Chapter Three: The Empty Room

Mr. Whitmore stood in the doorway, staring at the room that used to belong to his daughter. The walls were bare now, stripped of the posters and photographs that had once covered every inch. He could still hear her laughter if he closed his eyes.

"You should sell the house," Marlene said from behind him. She had followed him up the stairs, her footsteps quiet on the old wood. "There's nothing left for you here."

"I can't," Whitmore said. "Not yet."

Detective Inspector Reyes had called that morning with news about the case. The old murder from three years ago, the one everyone in town still whispered about, had a new lead. A witness had come forward. Someone who claimed to have seen Bill Foster near the harbor the night Giselle disappeared.

Marlene frowned. "Bill Foster is dead. He died last spring."

"I know," Whitmore said. "That's what makes it strange."

Down in the kitchen, Jamie was making coffee, the smell drifting up through the house like a promise of normalcy. Jamie had been staying with Whitmore since the funeral, helping him manage the estate, though everyone in town suspected there was more to it than simple kindness. Bull, the old family dog, padded in and settled at Jamie's feet, tail thumping against the tile.

"Something's wrong," Jamie called up the stairs. "Sergeant Rhodes is here. He says it's urgent."

Whitmore closed the door to his daughter's room gently, as if she were still sleeping inside, and went down to hear what the sergeant had to say. The threat, whatever it was, would not wait for grief.
