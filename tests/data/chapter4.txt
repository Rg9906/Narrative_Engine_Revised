Chapter 4: The Quiet Pool

Laurie paced the length of the study, the heavy oak floorboards creaking under his weight. His watch read 11:42 PM. The alibi he had so carefully constructed—the dinner at the club, the cab ride home at ten—was in ruins. The cab driver had remembered the detour to the apothecary. If the police found out, the timeline would place him squarely in the conservatory when the glass shattered.

He moved from the mahogany desk to the velvet armchair by the window. Outside, the rain drummed against the glass. The conservatory lay just across the courtyard, shrouded in darkness.

His gaze drifted to the silver tea tray he had abandoned hours ago. Beside it sat the glass milk bottle—the one he had specifically ordered sealed. But now, the wax seal was broken. The milk inside had a strange, iridescent sheen. The study door had been locked. How had the bottle been tampered with? 
Then, a chilling realization struck him. The servant's passage behind the bookshelf. It was an old architectural secret of the Whitmore house, a narrow corridor connecting the study directly to the kitchen pantry. Someone had used it to bypass the locked door and poison the milk.

Footsteps echoed in the hallway outside. Soft, deliberate. It was Talia. The sedative Dr. Aris had given her in the east wing should have kept her unconscious until dawn, but the sudden, sharp drop in barometric pressure from the storm often negated its effects. She must have slipped past Winnie, who always dozed off in the ante-room chair past eleven, and walked the length of the grand gallery to reach the study.

Laurie held his breath as the handle of the study door turned. Talia stepped in, her silk robe catching the dim light of the dying fire. She paused by the threshold, her eyes scanning the room until they locked onto him.

"You're still awake," she murmured, gliding toward the center of the room. She stopped exactly three paces from him, near the silver tea tray and the tainted milk bottle.

"I couldn't sleep," Laurie replied, trying to keep his voice steady. He took a step back, positioning himself between her and the desk drawer where he had hidden the key.

"I saw you earlier," she said, her voice barely above a whisper. "From my balcony. You weren't at the club at ten, Laurie. You were by the quiet pool."

The quiet pool. The small, secluded pond behind the conservatory. His heart hammered against his ribs. She knew. If she went to Inspector Vance...

Laurie's hand slowly moved behind his back, his fingers grazing the cold metal of the desk handle. Talia watched his movement, her gaze calculating. She took a step closer, reducing the distance between them to a mere two paces. The grandfather clock in the hall began to chime midnight.

"What are you going to do, Laurie?" she asked, the question hanging heavily in the suffocating air of the study.
